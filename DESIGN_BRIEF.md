# Design Brief — Auto Service Website Demo

## Context
A prospective client operates in car repair / automotive services and currently has no website. The team wants a shareable demo to show the potential visual direction, service catalogue, and online booking concept before a discovery meeting provides exact business details.

## Goal
Make the prospective client think: “This feels like a credible, well-designed website my customers would find easy to use.” The site should be persuasive through clarity and craft, not inflated claims.

## Audience
Vehicle owners who want to understand what the workshop can help with and request an appointment without friction.

## Brand status
**Unconfirmed.** Use a tasteful, clearly temporary brand name such as “Motive Auto Care — Concept Demo.” Never represent it as the client's official name. Make all copy editable.

## Visual personality
- Light, airy, calm and assured.
- Understated automotive editorial feeling, with a purposeful grid and considered typography.
- Clean white/off-white surfaces, charcoal/navy text, and one restrained accent color. Avoid large dark-background sections.
- Subtle borders and intentional spacing are preferable to repeated floating rounded cards.
- Premium line icons: consistent stroke, size and placement; use sparingly.
- Strong hierarchy and carefully selected imagery showing actual maintenance work, vehicle details or workshop environments.

## Initial token direction (suggestion, not a mandatory template)
- Background: `#FAFAF7`; surface: `#FFFFFF`; muted surface: `#F1F3F1`
- Primary text: `#1C2B30`; secondary text: `#52646A`
- Accent: `#285F72`; border: `#DCE3E2`
- Typography: clean contemporary sans for body, confident headings; establish a deliberate type scale.
- Radius: restrained, typically 6–12px; cards should be the exception, not the whole layout.
- Grid: comfortable content max width and generous whitespace.

These are starting points; make one coherent art direction, not a mixture of unrelated design trends.

## Suggested page hierarchy
1. **Header:** concept brand, concise navigation, distinct “Request appointment” action.
2. **Hero:** meaningful headline, short useful supporting copy, primary booking CTA, genuine automotive detail imagery (if rights/availability permit).
3. **Service offering:** 6 generic service examples, visually well-organized; not necessarily 6 identical cards.
4. **How it works:** simple 3-step explanation — choose service, share details/preferred date, workshop follows up (in a real deployment).
5. **Booking preview/experience:** functional demo form with honest messaging.
6. **Footer:** concept status, simple navigation, placeholder contact information with “To be confirmed.”

## Sample service copy (unverified as client offerings)
- Oil & filter change — Routine oil and filter service.
- Brake service — Inspection and repair requests for braking concerns.
- Engine diagnostics — Help identifying warning lights or performance issues.
- Battery service — Battery condition checks and replacement requests.
- Tire service — Tire checks, balancing and alignment inquiries.
- A/C service — Cabin cooling and ventilation-related checks.

## Booking interaction
- Step 1: service selection (or “Not sure”).
- Step 2: vehicle make/model/year (optional where appropriate), symptom/notes.
- Step 3: preferred date and time window. **Do not show fabricated available slots.**
- Step 4: name, email/phone with form validation and consent language if necessary.
- Step 5: clear review/confirmation screen stating: **“Demo request only — no appointment has been booked or sent.”**
- A real version would need backend, notifications, availability rules, privacy disclosures and spam protection; these are intentionally out of scope.

## Quality bar
- Recognisable, individually authored composition — not a generic hero + three cards + testimonial wall template.
- Typographic alignment, line lengths, whitespace and hover/focus states should look deliberate.
- No fake reviews, fake accreditation, false guarantees or fictional company statistics.
- High-quality iconography: aligned, consistent and limited to genuinely useful concepts.
- Mobile layout should be designed, not just compressed desktop.

## Avoid
Dark mode, flashy gradients, generic AI visuals, random decorative icons, stock supercar photos unrelated to servicing, unrealistic promises, placeholder lorem ipsum and nonworking navigation buttons.

## Pending discovery questions
Official name/logo; city and service area; real service list; contact channels; hours; supported vehicles; booking workflow (request vs confirmed slot); shop capacity; approvals; privacy/legal wording; photos; languages; maintenance/hosting responsibilities.
